# Matriz — Architecture Definition (v0.1)

The client has not yet approved the MVP scope ([OQ-016](constraints.md#open-questions)), and the [questions below](#questions-that-could-change-the-choice) may change the decision. This document names no technologies, frameworks, databases, or hosting options, assumes no scale targets, and implements nothing. Terminology follows the [glossary](requirements.md#glossary).

The MVP content is itself a proposal ([Assumption A-012](constraints.md#assumptions)). Candidates [outside the initial scope](requirements.md#outside-the-initial-scope) are not requirements; they serve only as an indication of possible growth when judging future evolution.

## Comparison of options

- **Monolith:** one deployable application with no enforced boundaries between its areas of responsibility.
- **Modular monolith:** one deployable application divided into modules with explicit boundaries; each module owns its rules and data and is used by the others only through its interface.
- **Microservices:** several independently deployable services, each owning its rules and data and collaborating with the others over a network.

| Criterion | Monolith | Modular monolith | Microservices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simplicity | Simplest: one codebase and one deployable unit; rules spanning projects, members, and tasks are checked in one place against one set of data. | Nearly as simple to build and run; adds the upfront effort of defining module boundaries while many domain rules are still unconfirmed. | Least simple: network communication, partial failures, and data split across services from the first release; rules spanning projects, members, and tasks need cross-service coordination when those concepts sit in different services. |
| Maintenance | Easy while the codebase is small; without enforced boundaries, coupling tends to grow and changes become riskier as features are added. | Changes and tests stay local to a module, and moving a boundary is an internal refactoring; keeping boundaries intact requires ongoing discipline. | Each service can be changed, tested, and released on its own; changes to rules spanning services touch several services and their contracts, and moving a boundary means moving data between services. |
| Operational cost | Lowest: one unit to deploy, monitor, back up, and secure. | Comparable to the monolith. | Highest: several units to deploy, monitor, secure, and keep compatible, plus the automation and skills this requires. |
| Future evolution | Grows, scales, and is released as one unit; extracting parts later is difficult if the internal structure has eroded. | Grows, scales, and is released as one unit; well-bounded modules can later be extracted into services if targets or team organization justify it. | Parts can be scaled, released, and isolated from each other's failures independently, and separate teams can work autonomously; these benefits matter only if targets or team organization require them, and both are unknown ([OQ-010](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-020](constraints.md#open-questions)). |
| Consistent permission enforcement ([OQ-019](constraints.md#open-questions)) | A single enforcement point is possible. | A single enforcement point is possible, shared by all modules. | Each service must apply the same membership and role rules or depend on a shared component; keeping them consistent takes additional effort. |

The comparison is qualitative and uses no scale figures because none are known ([OQ-010](constraints.md#open-questions)).

## Decision and rationale

**Decision.** Adopt a **modular monolith** for the proposed MVP: one deployable application divided into modules with explicit boundaries, described in [Components and responsibilities](#components-and-responsibilities).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted; provisional until the client approves the MVP scope ([OQ-016](constraints.md#open-questions)). |
| Decided by | The project team, on 2026-10-25. The [comparison](#comparison-of-options) supports the choice; the decision itself is human. |
| Record | This section is the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md#current-phase-and-next-steps) states that architecture decisions are recorded through ADRs, but no ADR location or numbering has been defined; this record can be moved into an ADR once they are defined. |
| Not decided | Technologies, frameworks, data storage, hosting, deployment, and how the User interface is delivered. |

**Rationale.**

1. **Rules span projects, members, and tasks.** Removing a participant depends on their assigned tasks, completing a project depends on its tasks' states, and an assignee must be a member of the task's project ([FR-005](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [FR-007](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [FR-010](requirements.md#functional-requirements); [Assumption A-004](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-007](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-009](constraints.md#assumptions)). Within one deployable unit these checks need no coordination between services; how simultaneous changes are resolved remains open ([OQ-017](constraints.md#open-questions)).
2. **One place to decide identity and permissions.** Every entry point relies on the same Identity and Access control modules for [NFR-001](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria) and [NFR-002](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria); how identity is established ([OQ-018](constraints.md#open-questions)) and how consistent enforcement is guaranteed ([OQ-019](constraints.md#open-questions)) remain open.
3. **Low operational cost while conditions are unknown.** No deadline, budget, or operating arrangement is known ([OQ-014](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-015](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-020](constraints.md#open-questions)); a single deployable unit keeps operational cost comparable to a monolith and below microservices.
4. **Unconfirmed rules are cheap to change.** Many rules await client answers ([OQ-002–OQ-008](constraints.md#open-questions)); moving a responsibility between modules is an internal refactoring, with no data to move between services.
5. **Room to evolve.** Unlike a plain monolith, explicit boundaries keep changes local as features are added, and a well-bounded module can later be extracted into a separate service if targets ([OQ-010](constraints.md#open-questions)) or team organization ([OQ-020](constraints.md#open-questions)) justify it.

**Main trade-offs.**

| Gained | Accepted in exchange |
|---|---|
| One unit to build, deploy, monitor, back up, and secure. | The application is scaled, deployed, and fails as one unit; a fault in one module can affect all of them. |
| Rules spanning modules are checked within one unit. | Modules interact only through their interfaces, which adds indirection compared with a plain monolith. |
| Clear ownership of rules and data per module. | Upfront effort to define boundaries while many rules are unconfirmed, and ongoing discipline to keep them, ideally verified automatically. |
| Boundaries can be moved by refactoring, and modules can be extracted later. | One release cadence for all modules, which parallel teams would have to coordinate ([OQ-020](constraints.md#open-questions)); a later extraction still requires splitting data and replacing internal calls with network communication. |

**Options not chosen.**

- **Monolith:** comparable simplicity and operational cost, but without enforced boundaries coupling tends to grow as features are added, making later changes and any extraction harder.
- **Microservices:** independent scaling, release, and team autonomy are not justified by any known target or team organization ([OQ-010](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-020](constraints.md#open-questions)), while their operational cost and the cross-service coordination of rules spanning projects, members, and tasks would apply from the first release.

## Components and responsibilities

Module names are descriptive. The rules each module applies are the proposed requirements and assumptions, not confirmed client decisions. No module is defined for candidates [outside the initial scope](requirements.md#outside-the-initial-scope) ([Assumption A-012](constraints.md#assumptions)).

| Module | Responsibilities | Data owned | Traceability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Establishes a person's identity before any project or task information is disclosed or changed; gives the other modules a stable reference to each person. The method, and how people receive initial access, are undecided. | The people known to the application and the information needed to identify them; content depends on the answers to OQ-011 and OQ-018. | [NFR-001](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria), [NFR-003](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria), [EX-012](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions); [Assumption A-014](constraints.md#assumptions); [OQ-001](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-011](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-018](constraints.md#open-questions) |
| Projects | Creates Active projects with a nonempty name, making the creator Project Owner and a member; renames Active projects; completes an Active project only when it has no unfinished tasks; lists the projects in which a person participates, including Completed ones; records members and their roles; adds participants and removes a non-owner participant only if they have no tasks assigned in the project; refuses changes to Completed projects. | Projects (name, state), their members, and each member's role. | [FR-001–FR-005](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [FR-007](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [EX-001](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-006](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-007](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-009](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-010](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions); [Assumption A-001](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-002](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-003](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-007](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-008](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-009](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-011](constraints.md#assumptions) |
| Tasks | Creates tasks with a nonempty title in Active projects, starting To do and possibly unassigned; changes titles; assigns a task to one member of its project; changes task states among To do, In progress, and Done without a required sequence, including reopening Done tasks; lists a project's tasks with their state and assignee; refuses changes when the project is not Active; tells Projects whether a project has unfinished tasks and whether a person has tasks assigned in a project. | Tasks (title, state, project, assignee). | [FR-006](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [FR-008–FR-011](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [EX-002](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-008](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-011](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-013](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions); [Assumption A-004](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-005](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-010](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-011](constraints.md#assumptions) |
| Progress | Derives a project's task counts by state and its percentage Done from the task states provided by Tasks; shows “No tasks” instead of a percentage when the project has no tasks. | Access control | Decides whether an identified person may list, view, or change a project or task, using membership and roles from Projects and assignments from Tasks; decides who may create projects once that rule is defined; a refused request discloses no project or task information and changes nothing. | None of its own; applies the permission rules to data owned by Projects and Tasks. | Permission parts of [FR-001–FR-011](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [NFR-002](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria), [EX-003](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-004](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions), [EX-013](user-stories.md#edge-cases-and-exceptions); [Assumption A-001](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-002](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-008](constraints.md#assumptions); [OQ-001](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-002](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-019](constraints.md#open-questions) |
| User interface (entry point) | Presents the MVP interactions; requires an established identity and an Access control decision before every read or change; shows the messages required by the acceptance criteria. [NFR-004–NFR-006](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria) (response time, availability, concurrent use) apply to the whole application because all modules are deployed together; their targets are unknown ([OQ-010](constraints.md#open-questions)). [NFR-003](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria) applies mainly to Identity, but also to the references to people held by Projects and Tasks ([OQ-011](constraints.md#open-questions)).

**Module rules.**

1. **Data ownership.** Each module owns its data and exposes it to other modules only through its interface; how and where data is stored is not decided.
2. **Acyclic dependencies.** Projects uses Identity to refer to people. Tasks uses Projects for project state and membership. Projects obtains the task facts it needs through an interface that it defines and Tasks implements, so Projects does not depend on Tasks. Progress uses Tasks. Access control uses Identity, Projects, and Tasks. The User interface uses Identity, Access control, and the modules it presents; no module depends on the User interface.
3. **Single access path.** Every read or change from an entry point is checked by Identity and then by Access control before it reaches Projects, Tasks, or Progress ([NFR-001](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria), [NFR-002](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria)). Guaranteeing this for every entry point, including any integration ([OQ-012](constraints.md#open-questions)), is part of [OQ-019](constraints.md#open-questions) and remains open.
4. **Rules spanning modules.** Removing a participant ([FR-005](requirements.md#functional-requirements)), completing a project ([FR-007](requirements.md#functional-requirements)), assigning a task ([FR-010](requirements.md#functional-requirements)), and refusing changes in a Completed project ([Assumption A-011](constraints.md#assumptions)) are evaluated within the single deployable unit, without network calls between modules; how simultaneous changes are resolved is open ([OQ-017](constraints.md#open-questions)).

## Questions that could change the choice

All of these questions are unresolved, and this document answers none of them. OQ-012 and OQ-017–OQ-020 are registered as blocking architecture.

| Open question | Unresolved | Effect on the decision or the modules |
|---|---|---|
| [OQ-010](constraints.md#open-questions) | Response-time, availability, concurrent-use, and accessibility targets ([NFR-004–NFR-007](requirements.md#non-functional-requirements-and-unresolved-quality-criteria)). | If some parts need different availability or capacity targets, extracting the affected module into a separate service could be justified; accessibility needs affect the User interface. |
| [OQ-012](constraints.md#open-questions) | Whether integrations with existing applications are required, and what information they exchange. | Each integration is a new entry point that must go through Identity and Access control; substantial integrations may warrant a dedicated integration module, and integrations that need independent deployment could reopen the choice. |
| [OQ-017](constraints.md#open-questions) | How simultaneous changes to the same project or task are resolved ([FR-003](requirements.md#functional-requirements), [FR-009–FR-011](requirements.md#functional-requirements)). | Resolved within the single unit, mainly in Projects and Tasks. Examples that need an answer: the Project Owner completes a project while the assignee reopens one of its tasks ([Assumption A-007](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-005](constraints.md#assumptions)); a participant is removed while a task is being assigned to them ([Assumption A-009](constraints.md#assumptions), [Assumption A-004](constraints.md#assumptions)). |
| [OQ-018](constraints.md#open-questions), with [OQ-001](constraints.md#open-questions) | How identity is established; who may create projects and how people receive initial access. | Identity either establishes identity itself or relies on an existing identity source; both fit the chosen style but change the module's content and the personal data it holds. The answer to OQ-001 defines the project-creation rule in Access control. |
| [OQ-019](constraints.md#open-questions) | How permissions are enforced consistently across every way of accessing or changing work. | Access control is the single decision point for every entry point; how this is guaranteed and verified remains open. |
| [OQ-016](constraints.md#open-questions) | Whether the client approves the MVP content, priorities, sufficiency, and exclusions. | Approval of the proposed scope removes the provisional status. Scope changes add, remove, or reshape modules; a substantially different scope could reopen the choice of style. |
| [OQ-002–OQ-008](constraints.md#open-questions) | Roles and permissions, naming rules, task states and transitions, the progress measure, completion and reopening, reassignment, and ownership changes. | Answers may change rules within modules or move responsibilities between them, for example ownership transfer in Projects, reassignment in Tasks, or a different measure or a cross-project overview in Progress; these are expected to be internal refactorings. |
| [OQ-011](constraints.md#open-questions) | Personal data processed, who may access it, and the applicable GDPR, retention, and internal obligations. | Personal data would sit mainly in Identity, with references to people in Projects and Tasks; obligations such as retention may add responsibilities not yet assigned to any module. |
| [OQ-014](constraints.md#open-questions), [OQ-015](constraints.md#open-questions) | Deadline and budget. | A tight deadline or a limited budget reinforces the choice of a single deployable unit. |
| [OQ-020](constraints.md#open-questions) | Who operates and maintains Matriz, and any hosting constraints. | Several teams working in parallel could justify extracting modules later; limited operating capacity reinforces the choice; hosting constraints may limit deployment options. |

OQ-020 was added to the [open questions](constraints.md#open-questions) register by this analysis, because the capacity to operate and maintain the application affects operational cost and future evolution.
